Why Does My Voice Sound Different in Recordings?

By Buğra Günay, maker of Fenno

Why your recorded voice sounds strange to you (bone conduction), why others hear the recording version, and how to get comfortable with it and improve how you sound with Fenno on iPhone.

Almost everyone dislikes their voice on a recording at first. It sounds higher, thinner or less confident than the voice in your head. There is a simple physical reason, and the recording is much closer to what other people hear.

Two paths to your ears

When you speak, sound reaches your inner ear through the air and also through the bones of your skull. Bone conduction carries lower frequencies well, so the voice you hear while speaking sounds deeper and fuller. A microphone only captures the air-conducted sound, which is what listeners hear.

Why it feels uncomfortable

You have heard your bone-conducted voice your whole life, so the recording feels like a stranger. The discomfort is about familiarity, not about the voice being worse. It fades with exposure.

Phones, video and calls

Phone microphones, compression and speaker quality change the sound further. On video calls and in social videos, people hear a version shaped by the microphone and room, so a quiet room and a microphone close to your mouth make a real difference.

Get used to it, then improve it

  1. Record yourself regularly for a minute about something you know well.
  2. Listen back without judging the tone; notice pace, pauses and filler words instead.
  3. Pick one thing to change at a time, such as slowing down or pausing between ideas.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my voice sound different in recordings?

Because you normally hear your voice through both air and skull bones, which adds low frequencies. A recording captures only the air-conducted sound, which is what other people hear.

Is my recorded voice what others hear?

Much closer than the voice in your head, though the microphone and speaker also color it.

How can I like my voice more?

Listen to recordings regularly so it becomes familiar, and work on things you can control, such as pace, pauses and clarity.
