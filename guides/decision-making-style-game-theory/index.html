What Is My Decision-Making Style? A Test You Play

By Buğra Günay, maker of MindType

The four classic decision-making styles (directive, analytical, conceptual, behavioral), why self-report quizzes miss your real style, and a game-based test on iPhone with MindType.

Most decision-making style quizzes ask you to rate statements about yourself. The problem is that people describe how they would like to decide, not how they actually decide under pressure. A behavior-based test shows the second.

The four classic decision-making styles

A widely taught model by Alan Rowe sorts decision makers on two axes: tolerance for ambiguity, and focus on tasks or on people.

  • Directive: low tolerance for ambiguity, task-focused. Fast, practical, relies on rules and experience.
  • Analytical: high tolerance for ambiguity, task-focused. Gathers data, weighs options, can be slow.
  • Conceptual: high tolerance for ambiguity, people-focused. Big-picture, creative, long-term.
  • Behavioral: low tolerance for ambiguity, people-focused. Seeks agreement and harmony, listens to others.

Why a quiz is not enough

Self-report answers are shaped by how we want to be seen. Economists and psychologists use small games instead, such as the Prisoner's Dilemma, Ultimatum and Trust games, because choices with consequences reveal cooperation, fairness, trust and risk more honestly than ratings do.

The games and what they reveal

  • Prisoner's Dilemma: cooperation when trust is uncertain.
  • Ultimatum game: how much you value fairness.
  • Trust game: how much you trust and reciprocate.
  • Biases such as loss aversion and anchoring: where your choices drift.

Take a test you play

MindType is a personality test for iPhone built on these games. Each takes about three minutes. Your choices are mapped across 10 dimensions, including cooperation, trust, risk, fairness, patience and adaptability, and matched to one of 6 decision archetypes such as Strategic Maximizer, Social Harmonizer and Cautious Analyzer. Keep it in perspective

Decision styles change with context, stress and stakes. Treat any result as a mirror for reflection; MindType is for self-discovery and education, not a clinical assessment.
