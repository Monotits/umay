The Ultimatum Game Explained: Fairness vs. Self-Interest

By Buğra Günay, maker of MindType

How the Ultimatum game works, what game theory predicts, what people actually do, why low offers get rejected and what your choices reveal about fairness. Play it on iPhone with MindType.

In the Ultimatum game, one player, the proposer, is given an amount of money and offers a split to a second player, the responder. If the responder accepts, both get the split. If the responder rejects, both get nothing.

What theory predicts

If both only care about money, the responder should accept any offer above zero, since something beats nothing. Knowing that, the proposer should offer the smallest possible amount.

What people actually do

In experiments since the early 1980s, people rarely behave that way. Proposers commonly offer between 40 and 50 percent, and responders often reject offers below roughly 20 to 30 percent, giving up money to punish what they see as unfairness. Results vary across cultures and stakes, but the pattern is robust.

Why people reject free money

  • Fairness: an unequal split feels insulting.
  • Reciprocity: punishing a greedy proposer enforces norms.
  • Emotion: anger at a low offer can outweigh the small gain.

Ultimatum vs. Dictator game

In the Dictator game the responder cannot reject. Offers drop, but many people still give something, which separates real generosity from fear of rejection.

What your choices reveal

As proposer, your offer shows how you balance self-interest and fairness. As responder, your threshold shows how much you are willing to pay to resist unfair treatment.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Ultimatum game?

A two-player game where one proposes how to split money and the other accepts or rejects. Rejection leaves both with nothing.

Why do people reject offers in the Ultimatum game?

Because they see low offers as unfair and are willing to give up money to punish them.

What is a typical offer in the Ultimatum game?

Experiments commonly find offers of 40 to 50 percent, with offers below about 20 to 30 percent often rejected.

What is the difference between the Ultimatum and Dictator games?

In the Dictator game the receiver cannot reject the offer, so it measures generosity without the threat of rejection.
